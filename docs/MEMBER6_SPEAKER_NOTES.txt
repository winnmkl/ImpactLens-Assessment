--- SLIDE 8 (~1 minute) ---

"Hi, I'm [Name]. I designed the risk calculation engine in ImpactLens.

Every asset starts with two inputs from the assessor: Probability from 1 to 5,
and Severity from 1 to 5. We also compute a CIA score by adding Confidentiality,
Integrity, and Availability - each scored 1 to 3. That total drives the
classification band: Public, Internal Use, Confidential, or Restricted.

Before we apply any security controls, the system calculates INHERENT risk -
the worst-case exposure. I implemented NIST SP 800-30 style escalations. For
example: if the asset is Internet-facing and the threat is external cyber,
probability is bumped up and cannot stay below 3. If the asset processes PII
or SPI and the threat is a data leak or DPA breach, severity is forced to 5.
Restricted data - CIA 8 or higher - with a cyber or insider threat pushes
severity to at least 4. Financial assets under PCI scope force severity 5 for
any cyber threat.

After those rules run, we look up the rating on a 5-by-5 matrix - the same
heat map you see in the Risk Register. That gives us Very Low, Low, Moderate,
or High INHERENT risk. This is the risk picture if nothing mitigating existed."

--- SLIDE 9 (~1 minute) ---

"Part two is RESIDUAL risk - after controls.

We have 13 organization-level controls, from documented procedures through MFA,
encryption, firewall, and incident response. Not every control applies to every
threat. I built a controlMap so only threat-relevant controls mathematically
reduce probability or severity. Each control has weights - for example MFA has
a strong probability weight, while backup mainly reduces severity because it
limits impact after an incident.

When certain pairs are both active, synergy bonuses apply - like RBAC plus MFA,
or vulnerability management plus EDR plus IRP. But I also added diminishing
returns using a saturation curve, so users cannot game the system by checking
every box and dropping risk to the minimum.

The last layer is mandatory floors. Depending on the asset - Restricted class,
PII, Internet-facing, or Financial for PCI - specific controls must be in place.
If any are missing, residual risk cannot fall below Moderate, or High for PCI.
The UI shows exactly which controls are missing and cites ISO, NIST, CIS, or
RA 10173.

Finally, risk appetite enforcement: if residual is High, the treatment dropdown
blocks Accept - aligned with ISO 27001. That is the logic I implemented - inherent
risk shows exposure, residual shows what is left after controls and compliance,
and floors make sure standards cannot be bypassed. Thank you."

WALKTHROUGH EXAMPLE (USE IF PANEL ASKS OR YOU HAVE 30 SEC EXTRA)
================================================================================

Scenario: PLM Online Tuition Payment Gateway (type FA, Internet Facing)
Threat: cyber_ext_leak (data leak)
User picks P=3, S=4 initially.

STEP 1 - Escalations:
  - Internet Facing + cyber_ext -> P = max(3, 3+1) = 4 (capped at 5)
  - FA has PII/SPI from profile -> if leak threat, S = 5
  - FA + cyber -> S = 5 (PCI)
  Adjusted: P=4, S=5 -> Matrix 5-4 -> INHERENT = High

STEP 2 - Controls (relevant for cyber_ext_leak: 1,3,4,7,9,10,12):
  User checks: RBAC(3), MFA(4), Encryption(7), Firewall(10)
  Weights sum on P and S, plus RBAC+MFA synergy (+0.5 on P)
  Diminishing returns applied -> residual P and S drop
  Example outcome might be Moderate or Low on paper

STEP 3 - Mandatory floors (FA triggers PCI set: MFA, Encryption, Vuln, Segmentation):
  If Vuln(11) or Segmentation(12) NOT checked -> floor = High
  Residual cannot be below High even if math says Low

STEP 4 - Treatment:
  If residual stays High -> Accept is disabled

Say in one line:
  "Even with MFA and encryption, missing PCI-required segmentation keeps
   residual at High until the gap is closed."

================================================================================
WHY I BUILT IT THIS WAY (1-2 SENTENCES IF ASKED "WHY")
================================================================================

  - Threat-relevant only: NIST SP 800-30 says controls should match the threat
    source - checking physical locks should not reduce ransomware probability.

  - Mandatory separate from relevant: A DDoS threat might not need RBAC for
    math, but Restricted-class data still REQUIRES RBAC by ISO - so both paths
    enable the checkbox.

  - Diminishing returns: Prevents unrealistic "check all 13 = Very Low" scores
    that auditors would reject.

ANTICIPATED Q&A - SHORT ANSWERS
================================================================================

Q: Where is the formula?
A: In assets/scripts/app.js - functions calculateRiskMath() and
   getApplicableMandatorySets(). It runs live when the form changes.

Q: Is the 5x5 matrix custom?
A: It follows the PLM IAR template - INHERIT lookup table keyed by severity-
   probability pairs, same as our Risk Guidelines screen.

Q: Why block Accept on High?
A: ISO 27001:2022 section 6.1.3 - accepting High residual is outside normal
   risk appetite; treatment must be Mitigate, Transfer, or Avoid.

Q: What if Info Sec disagrees with the number?
A: They can adjust P, S, threat, environment, and controls - the engine
   recalculates instantly. Reject reasons can cite "escalation unjustified."

Q: Does the database recalculate too?
A: The browser calculates on edit; master_setup.sql has il_inherit() helper
   for seed alignment. Saved values are prob, sev, inherit, residual on Assets.
